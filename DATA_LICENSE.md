# Data licensing and attribution

EQ Library contains application software and consumes public EQ data from multiple qualified sources. The software license does not replace the terms, attribution, or provenance of third-party EQ data.

## Application software

The application source code, tests, and project documentation are distributed under the Apache License, Version 2.0. See `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.

The ToneBoosters/UAPP normalization and XML mapping is based in part on `SiliconExarch/EqConverter`, licensed under Apache-2.0. The native Kotlin implementation is ported from the established `weekssa/opra-uapp-converter` behavior; provenance is preserved in `NOTICE`.

## Runtime catalog and source-specific rights

The app ships with zero headphones. Normal operation downloads the validated live canonical catalog and the OPRA compatibility/base catalog, validates them, and caches last-known-good data locally.

The canonical catalog is a provenance-preserving compilation of structured EQ records from separately qualified source lanes. Rights and attribution requirements remain source-specific. `config/source_registry.json` records the maintained source identity, redistribution policy, attribution requirement, and license/access notes for each lane; `docs/SOURCE_INGESTION_STRATEGY.md` defines qualification, provenance, deduplication, and publication policy.

EQ Library publishes structured EQ parameters and minimal source/provenance metadata needed to identify and reproduce a tuning. It does not claim a blanket license over third-party prose, images, measurements, or other unrelated source material, and those materials are not republished merely because they helped establish provenance.

## OPRA-derived data and generated presets

Headphone/product/EQ data consumed from OPRA is licensed by OPRA under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-SA 4.0).

The Android runtime reads the supported OPRA distribution feed at `https://opra.roonlabs.net/database_v1.jsonl`. OPRA remains the complete compatibility/base layer while qualified canonical records and provenance can be overlaid without silently shrinking the OPRA library.

Generated ToneBoosters/UAPP XML presets that reproduce or convert selected OPRA dataset material retain the applicable OPRA attribution and, to the extent copyright or database rights apply to OPRA-derived portions, remain subject to CC BY-SA 4.0 rather than the application's Apache-2.0 software license.

The app preserves available OPRA profile identity, creator/author, details, source link, IDs, acoustic parameters, and generated-preset state. When OPRA creator information is missing, the approved literal label `Creator information missing` is used rather than inventing an identity.

## AutoEq-derived structured EQ data

AutoEq software is MIT licensed, while the underlying measurements used by AutoEq originate from multiple third-party measurement sources with their own provenance and terms.

EQ Library republishes only qualified structured equalizer coefficients generated by AutoEq, retains upstream measurement-source and rig/context provenance, and does not treat AutoEq's software license as blanket permission to redistribute raw third-party measurement data. It also does not label an AutoEq-generated result as if the measurement provider authored that EQ.

Current exact source-policy details are maintained in `config/source_registry.json`.

## Qualified MIT-licensed creator/repository sources

The canonical catalog may include exact structured EQ parameters from specifically qualified MIT-licensed repositories. Current maintained examples include:

- MrChillStorm Headphone Target — only exact profiles enumerated by the qualified-source manifest;
- `jurf/fairbuds` — only explicitly qualified author-origin Fairbuds profiles, excluding third-party-measurement-derived AutoEq outputs from that repository;
- `wabsto1/ParaEQ` — qualified source-authored General EQ presets; and
- `MilcioSSQ/eq` — qualified source-authored General EQ presets.

The source registry and qualified manifests define the exact publication boundary and preserve source/creator attribution. Repository-wide licensing must not be used to broaden that boundary beyond the files and provenance actually qualified by the project.

The MIT license text applicable to a qualified source remains available from that source repository; catalog/source metadata preserves the originating repository and attribution needed to identify it.

## Public community and repository EQs

Qualified public forums, GitHub repositories/Gists, and other community lanes may contribute exact structured PEQ coefficients when the source is public, attribution/provenance is traceable, access policy permits the maintained retrieval path, and the record passes parsing, identity, source-policy, deduplication, and living-archive validation.

EQ Library preserves creator/username when available, original source URL, and exact structured EQ values. It stores only minimal tuning context needed for provenance and does not republish unrelated forum prose, screenshots, images, or raw measurement data.

A community EQ being publicly visible does not cause EQ Library to claim ownership of it. Source-specific policy and attribution remain attached to the record. Sources with no compliant automated access path remain paused rather than being scraped or bypassed; current paused/active state is maintained in `config/source_registry.json`.

## Squiglink-compatible measurement provenance

Squiglink/CrinGraph-compatible measurement sources are treated as measurement/provenance surfaces, not as source-authored PEQ catalogs. EQ Library does not silently choose a target, convert measurement curves into invented filters, or republish raw measurement data through this lane.

Exact PEQ derived from those measurements may enter through a separately qualified carrier such as AutoEq, with measurement-source provenance retained.

## User-local EQs

Personal EQs imported, created, recovered, or captured from a supported DAC remain local user data. They are not submitted to or published in the canonical catalog merely because they are stored in EQ Library.

## Generated presets and combined provenance

Generated files are transformations of the selected EQ data into a target format. Source attribution and applicable source terms continue to apply to source-derived portions of those files; EQ Library's Apache-2.0 software license does not relicense third-party EQ data.

Where one acoustic tuning has multiple matching source records, EQ Library may deduplicate the tuning while retaining multiple provenance links. Deduplication does not erase or replace the underlying source attribution.
